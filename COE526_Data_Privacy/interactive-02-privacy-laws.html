Roles, Electronic Communications, and Transfers

Keep scope, role, and transfer mechanism separate. These distinctions correct three common mistakes in system requirements.

European Union ePrivacy Directive

Directive 2002/58/EC, as amended by Directive 2009/136/EC, focuses on privacy in electronic communications, including confidentiality of communications and rules affecting cookies or access to terminal equipment. It is not the rule that authorizes every international transfer.

General Data Protection Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 governs processing of personal data more broadly. It addresses legal bases, principles, rights, controllers and processors, security, breach duties, impact assessments, and international transfers.

U.S. privacy is sectoral and increasingly state-based

The United States has federal laws for sectors or data types—such as HIPAA for covered health information, the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act for financial institutions, the Fair Credit Reporting Act for consumer reports, the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act for certain online services directed to children, and the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act for education records—alongside a growing set of comprehensive state privacy laws.

From Legal Rule to System Design

A privacy requirement should affect the full information life cycle and leave evidence that the design works.

Capture

Define purpose and legal basis, minimize fields, provide notice, and obtain valid consent where consent is the basis.

Store

Inventory data, restrict access, encrypt appropriately, define retention, and govern processor contracts.

Use and share

Enforce purpose and use limits, record disclosures, handle subject requests, and review international transfers.

Destroy

Delete or anonymize on schedule, propagate deletion to copies where required, and retain evidence of disposal.

Engineering readiness checklist

  • Maintain a data inventory and name owners.
  • Run a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) or Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) when risk warrants it.
  • Use privacy by design and privacy-protective defaults.
  • Build access, correction, objection, erasure, and portability workflows where applicable.
  • Detect, assess, document, and notify breaches under the applicable rule.
  • Test retention, deletion, processor, and transfer controls rather than relying on policy text.

Generative artificial intelligence and large language models

Training data

Web scraping may collect personal data without a clear legal basis or adequate transparency. Inventory sources, remove unnecessary personal data, and document the basis and limitations.

Erasure and memorization

Deleting a source row does not necessarily remove memorized content from model weights. Prevention, privacy testing, and machine-unlearning research do not yet provide a universal deletion mechanism.

Prompts and logs

Source code, health records, or customer data pasted into a model may create new processing and retention. Use approved services, minimize input, scrub identifiers, and configure retention.

Accuracy and correction

False statements about a real person create accuracy and redress problems. Ground outputs in reliable sources, expose uncertainty, keep citations, and provide a correction path.
