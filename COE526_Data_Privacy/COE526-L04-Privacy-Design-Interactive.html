01 · Legal idea → engineering decision

GDPR Article 25: Data Protection by Design and by Default

Article 25 calls for appropriate technical and organizational measures to implement data-protection principles. This section connects legal language to engineering choices.

What Article 25 requires:

1. Implement from the start

Privacy is an engineering requirement, not an afterthought. It shapes the system from conception.

2. Make it the default

Out-of-the-box settings must reflect the most privacy-protective option. Users should not have to opt in to privacy.

3. Document and verify

Record what you did, why you did it, and how you tested it. Evidence matters when regulators review your decisions.

03 · Operationalizing privacy

Privacy Engineering Across the Life Cycle

Privacy engineering runs with the system lifecycle. Each phase has distinct privacy responsibilities.

Conception

Threat model. Define data flows, actors, and what privacy means for this system.

Specification

Write privacy requirements. Name what behavior you expect and how to verify it.

Design

Choose techniques. Encryption, access control, data minimization, retention policies.

Implementation

Code. Translate requirements into architecture, APIs, and configurations.

Testing

Verify compliance. Privacy unit tests, integration tests, and manual security review.

Deployment

Default settings. Make privacy the out-of-box state. Train operators.

Operation

Monitor. Log access, detect anomalies, respond to requests, audit controls.
