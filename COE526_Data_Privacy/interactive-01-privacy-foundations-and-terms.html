What must a privacy analysis distinguish?

Lecture 1B moves from the breadth of privacy to concrete data, actors, attacks, and claims. These distinctions prevent “remove the names” from being treated as a complete privacy method.

Personal data is broader than direct identifiers

Names, contact details, device identifiers, locations, records, and inferences may identify or relate to a person. “Personally Identifiable Information (PII)” is common U.S. terminology, while each law defines its own scope.

Sensitive personal data is a legal subset

Health, genetic, biometric, religious, or similar data may receive extra protection depending on the applicable law. Children’s data needs special care, but age alone is not a General Data Protection Regulation special category.

Privacy depends on context and perspective

A company, data subject, analyst, privacy officer, insider, outsider, and attacker may see different evidence and face different incentives. State whose perspective defines the claim.
