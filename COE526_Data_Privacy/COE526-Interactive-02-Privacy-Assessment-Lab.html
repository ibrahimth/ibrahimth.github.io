Step 1 · Screen

Does this need a full assessment?

Tick every signal you can see in the case. The law sets the legal threshold; this screen also gives you a reason you can write down and defend.

This list is not complete. One signal can be enough on its own. Whichever way you decide, write down why.
Step 2 · Map

Describe what the system does, before you score it

Keep why separate from how. Follow the data all the way through: collected, guessed at, used, stored, shared, deleted.

Step 3 · Choose and rate risks

Write three risks that name a real person

Each risk must join four things: what the system does, the setting, who is affected, and what goes wrong for them. Cover normal operation as well as things going wrong.

Step 4 · Treat and re-score

Treat the cause, prove it works, then score what is left

Try to remove or replace the feature, collect less by default, or keep data on the device — before you fall back on a notice, a contract or training. The score that matters is the one left over after the control works.

Step 5 · Decide

Decide, using the risk that is left over

A score cannot accept risk — only a person can. Name who decides, on what conditions, with what evidence, and what would reopen it.

Step 6 · Defend the decision

Submit, compare, and explain yourself

Under the facts given, one answer fits best. In real work a different answer can also be defensible — if the different evidence and assumptions are written down.
